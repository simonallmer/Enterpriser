# Enterpriser — Style Doctrine

> *The light goes on.*

## 1. The idea

Enterpriser borrows the word from 1950s American business talk (Simon's reference: Alan Greenspan's use of
"enterpriser" for the entrepreneur). It borrows the mood of the same decade: the **naive optimism of the
machine age**, when a computer was a cabinet you could walk around and an aluminium skyscraper was front-page news.

We bring that optimism back for a new machine age. Robots are getting hands. The question is no longer
*"How do I manage fifty people?"* It is *"What will I craft with the hands of a machine?"*

### Five beliefs, from the reference pages

| Reference | What it teaches | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The robot sculptor** (mannequin carving a crankshaft in an attic studio) | The machine as craftsman, not a threat. Studio, not office. | **Emblem of the title.** Show machines *making* things, at human scale, in workshops. |
| **Univac 120 ad** ("Does Univac 120 really think?") | A computer is a real object: cabinets, panels, tubes. Bold question headline, one underlined word, three justified columns. | **Machines are objects, never clouds.** Headlines may ask a bold question. |
| **The woman and the lamp array** | Hundreds of small lights: the moment an idea switches on. | **The bulb array is Enterpriser's symbol.** Logo, dark-mode switch, age bars, timeline nodes. |
| **The factory cutaway** | Open the walls and show every floor at work. | **Cutaway honesty.** Explain how things are made by opening them up: diagrams, sections, floors. |
| **Alcoa "31 stories of aluminum"** | The material as hero. Condensed headline, serif body copy, three tick marks as separators. | **The material and the method are the story.** Never the money. |

### What we never show
Stacks of cash, stock tickers, rich-list portraits, conference stages, laptops in cafés, abstract "cloud" or
network-blob graphics, anonymous handshake stock photos. Innovation for its own sake, not as a route to an IPO.

## 2. Colour

Lithographic, slightly warm, saturated but never neon. Every colour is taken from the references.

| Token | Name | Hex | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | Paper Cream | `#F1E8D3` | the ad pages |
| `--ink` | Press Ink | `#1E2023` | body copy |
| `--teal` | Univac Teal | `#1E6A5E` | Univac backdrop |
| `--midnight` | Midnight Tube | `#252660` | vacuum-tube glow |
| `--blue` | Univac Blue | `#2E7DB5` | "UNIVAC 120" headline |
| `--bulb` | Bulb Glow | `#F7E58A` | lamp array |
| `--mustard` | Mustard Dress | `#DDA22C` | operator's dress |
| `--red` | Factory Red | `#B5392A` | cutaway floors, Alcoa canopy |
| `--green` | Wainscot Green | `#2F6A3E` | sculptor's attic |
| `--steel` | Aluminium | `#A7B3B8` | Alcoa panels, Univac cabinets |

**Light mode = lights on** (paper and ink). **Dark mode = lights off**: the teal-midnight of the Univac
backdrop, where the bulbs glow brightest. The switch is a light bulb, never a sun or moon.

## 3. Type

| Role | Face | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark, display questions | **Archivo Expanded Black** (width 125, weight 900) | "DOES UNIVAC 120 REALLY THINK?" |
| Section headlines, labels | **Archivo Condensed Bold** (width 62–75) | "31 STORIES OF ALUMINUM MAKE NEWS" |
| Body copy | **Libre Caslon Text** | justified serif copy in both ads |
| Data, numbers, IDs | **Courier Prime** | punched-card printouts, typewritten specs |

Rules: **never more than three typefaces** (Archivo, Libre Caslon Text, Courier Prime). No script faces: bylines
use Caslon italic, sign-offs use the Archivo wordmark. Headlines in capitals. One word per headline may be
underlined or set in Univac Blue.

## 4. Graphic devices

1. **The bulb array.** A grid of round lamps, some lit. Used as a dot-matrix marquee ("BUILD"), as age
   bars in the Index (one bulb = one century), and as timeline nodes.
2. **Floating numerals.** Coloured digits and symbols (7, Σ, λ, ÷, 5+) drifting over dark panels, as in the Univac ad.
3. **Stipple and grain.** A light print texture on illustrated panels, never on body text.
4. **Cutaway frames.** Irregular openings with a cream outline, showing coloured "floors" at work.
5. **Punched card.** Entry headers are cream cards with a clipped corner and faint columns of digits.
6. **Spec plate.** Key facts sit on an aluminium plate with four rivets.
7. **Green-bar paper.** Long tables use the alternating stripes of computer printout paper.
8. **Three ticks** (`/ / /`) in Factory Red separate thoughts, as in the Alcoa copy.
9. **The ad layout.** Illustration on top → question headline → bold lead → three justified columns → footer bar
   with the address left and the wordmark as logotype.

### Logo
A **vacuum tube** with a glowing filament, beside the wordmark ENTERPRISER (Archivo Expanded Black). No subtitle.
The tube is the machine made visible; its filament is the light going on. The single light bulb is reserved for the
lights-on/lights-off switch.

## Engine Room (basement)

The machinery under the four floors, in two separate collections:

- **Learning Curve**: system models in teaching order. Step 1 The Bicycle Shop (one stock, competing balancing
  loops, delays: the thermostat in business clothes), Step 2 The Oil Economy, Step 3 The Fishery. Each step links
  to the next; each uses everyday business examples rather than abstract ones.
- **Picture Statistics**: current data counted in symbols (first: One Square Metre). Kept apart from the models.

- **Emblem:** the shipping container (1956). Trade flow, a physical product, a standard unit.
- **System models** follow Donella Meadows' stock-and-flow notation, in colour: tanks (stocks), pipes with
  faucets (flows), clouds (model edge), thin arrows (information links), red **R** and blue **B** loop markers.
  Liquid width = flow rate; faucet handles turn with flow. Every part is clickable and explains itself.
  Models are our own simplifications; we cite the book, never reproduce its figures or text.
- **Picture statistics**: count symbols, never scale them. One symbol = one fixed quantity. The symbol
  shows the thing counted. Charts stay flat; 3D is reserved for system models you can spin.
- **3D dioramas** sit on a museum turntable with an underground cutaway (soil strata). Fixed doctrine colours,
  cream placards for labels, see-through pipes with flowing particles, ball valves that turn open. Where a stock
  is made of things, count them (one pumpjack = 10 units of capital). Idle sway around the front view; drag to spin.

## 5. Illustration

Type and colour carry the system, but **illustration is the soul of this style**. The 1950s look cannot be
faked with fonts alone. Budget for original painted illustration (gouache or airbrush look, stippled shadows,
saturated colour, people and machines at work).

First commissions:
1. **"The Enterpriser's Studio"**: masthead illustration. A robot craftsman at a workbench under a skylight,
   shaping an object; city rooftops outside. Our own composition, not a copy of the reference.
2. **Department vignettes** (one per floor): a planning table, a factory floor, a machine's hand, an enterpriser's workshop.
3. **Index cutaways**: one cutaway drawing per featured company, showing how its product is made.

## 6. Voice

- Headlines: a bold question or a plain claim. *"Can a robot carve a crankshaft?"* *"31 stories of aluminium make news."*
- Body: short sentences. Steps where possible. Concrete nouns: materials, machines, numbers.
- Optimistic, never ironic. Curious, never cynical.
- Global. A Japanese inn of 1,300 years sits above a trillion-dollar company.

## 7. Don'ts

- No sun/moon icons. No gradients-for-the-sake-of-gradients. No glassmorphism.
- No sans-serif body copy. No monospace except data.
- No blue-purple SaaS palette. No drop-shadow cards floating on white.
- No money as the hero.
